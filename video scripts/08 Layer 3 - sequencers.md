## Narration only

Layer three: sequencers. Record a pattern, and it plays back in time.

The step recorder has four tracks of sixteen or thirty-two steps. The top four rows are the tracks. The bottom four rows are keyboards.

Tap Board Record. It blinks red. While the clock is stopped, this is step record: every note or chord you press fills the next step, and the cursor moves on.

Tap Rest for an empty step. Tap Tie to hold the previous note through the next step.

Switch record off and press Play. Track one plays what you entered.

While the clock runs, record is live: whatever you play lands in the playing step.

Double-tap the edit key and the track rows become faders, one per step: velocity, probability, and ratchet. Each step can be different.

Page two is the classic drum grid. Each row is a lane: the bottom row is note thirty-six. Tap a pad to switch a step on.

Press Play, and a coloured column runs along. Two keys on the right switch between steps one to eight and nine to sixteen.

Page three makes Euclidean rhythms: you choose how many hits and the board spreads them as evenly as possible. Tap up or down on the arrows to pick a lane. Hold Hits and tap right to add hits. Hold Length and tap left to shorten the lane. Let go, and left and right move the lane's start point, the orange step.

Page four sequences knob movements instead of notes. Four lanes of eight steps. Not recording, the pads are live faders. Press Record, tap a step, then tap one of its eight pads to set the value; double-tap a step to switch it on or off. Press Play, and the values go to your synth in time. Row nine holds eight effect layers: tap one to select it, tap again to switch it on, and every layer that is on plays at once. Tap Learn, move a knob on your synth, and the lane takes that CC.

Four sequencers. They run on the internal clock, or follow Ableton's clock by themselves; the clock key on the side switches to the internal clock only.
