## Narration only

Before the keyboard can make music, it needs new firmware. Firmware is the small program inside the keyboard. Do not worry: this is safe, and I will show you the safety net first.

You need three things: the flash file, the flash tool folder, and the firmware file that ends in dot bin. They are all in the project folder.

Double-click Flash C100. It lists your firmware files, newest first. Type the number of the one you want and press Enter.

Now put the keyboard in flash mode. Unplug it, hold the top-left key, plug it back in, and let go after three seconds. The flasher finds the keyboard by itself.

Do not unplug while it says Flashing. When it says Done, the keyboard restarts on its own.

The keys now light up. This is the new firmware.

In Ableton, open Preferences, then the MIDI tab. Find the C100 and switch on Track and Remote. Track lets it play instruments. Remote lets it move knobs and map controls.

If a firmware ever does not start, the keyboard is not broken. Inside the keyboard there is a tiny reset button. Hold it while you plug the cable in, and the keyboard goes into flash mode by itself, and you can flash a good firmware again.

Keep one rule: only flash firmware you built or trust. Episode twelve shows how to build your own.

Press a pad. The MIDI light in Ableton flashes, and the drum rack answers. You are connected.

In the next episode we will learn the control keys, so you can move around the board with confidence.
