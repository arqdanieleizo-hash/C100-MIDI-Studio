## Narration only

Layer two turns the board into a control surface for Ableton.

Page one is a clip launcher. The eight by eight grid launches clips. Row two holds the track buttons. The side keys choose what they do.

To map it, press Control M in Ableton, click a clip, press the pad you want, and repeat. Close map mode when you are done. The pads send notes zero to sixty-three on channel sixteen.

Tap Arm, then a track button: that track is armed. Select takes you back.

Tap Volume, and the grid becomes eight faders. Tap higher for louder. Pan and the two sends work the same way. The bottom keys of the side columns are Ableton commands: undo, redo, record, quantize, and launch selected.

Page two is a Kaoss-pad style X Y effect. Touch the grid: left to right is X, bottom to top is Y.

There are eight effects, and each has its own MIDI channel, nine to sixteen. So every effect can control something different in Ableton.

Two fingers give the point between them. Hold keeps the effect on after you let go.

Page three: eight vertical faders for the macros of a rack. A good page when your other hand plays a piano.

Page five runs Ableton without the mouse. The top rows hold shortcuts for views, tracks, loop, play and zoom; the bottom rows hold editing shortcuts.

Page four is thirty-two knobs, always on, two keys each. The left key turns a knob down, the right key turns it up. Hold a key to keep turning. Rows seven to ten are C C twenty to thirty-five, and the top rows are the next sixteen.

Map any knob in Ableton the same way you mapped the clips: map mode, click a parameter, turn the knob.

The launcher, faders, X Y, shortcuts and knobs. Next: sequencers.
